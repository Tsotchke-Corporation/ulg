## The gate (section 8): why near-real-time Iron Ice is not a tuning job
- The budget for 1,000 steps/s is ~1 ms per step for all GPU work plus rendering on one shared queue.
- Fusion does not remove most per-step GPU work: two radix sorts per epoch rebuild every step (the exactness contract requires same-step positions); reaction placement at 150-250 capacity-sized dispatches even when nothing reacts (inferred); contact; thermal's all-pairs search; ~54 indirect dispatches (since reduced to ~14).
- Tier0's 1,100-1,260 steps/s is not like-for-like: no contact graph, no field contact, one row per particle (Iron Ice runs 4: 1,696 particles -> 6,784 rows).
- "Every law on" is ill-defined: the far-field "laws" (radiation, plasma, gas summary) are scaled-gravity placeholders (`wgsl.js:17194-17209`); the two-level route runs only on cesium-fluorine.
- Proposal: staged gates measured after each step with the Step 0 method; rate counts reactions and transfers actually committed. Candidate numbers then: ~13 steps/s after decoupling (reached: 19.5-41), the route's ~35 steps/s ceiling after contact (reached ~40 unaudited), then "a per-step GPU budget that the user sets" after fusion.
- dt is a lever: stiffness is already scaled to the carrier dt; doubling dt halves the steps/s needed. (This is what the Eshkol larger-dt slice pursued; it reached real time on water-realtime only.)

## Other findings (section 10): a backlog nobody has scheduled
Possible bugs (need a test; mostly reader-reported or inferred):
- Phantom product mass: for non-physical reactions `physical_reaction_pair_applied` always returns true, so the event kernel may book unplaced product mass with no reactants consumed (`wgsl.js:2890`, `:6892-7008`).
- The law-queue contact gate looks indexed by particle while the queue is ordered by active node.
- Moved-list overflow (512 slots) cuts off that pass's state copy: corrections probably lost at impacts.
- A heavy particle can be claimed by two three-body blocks in one pass (last write wins).
- Latent ReferenceErrors: `refreshMlsMpmGridUpdate` references an out-of-scope `executionGeneration` (`sphPhaseScene.js:36274`); a default parameter at `sphPhaseScene.js:10113`.
- A missing thermal-environment wall face defaults to a 0 K reservoir (`thermalEnvironmentAuthority.js:170-179`).
- Wave speed has two definitions (p-wave in the material table, bulk-only in `sphPhaseDemo.js:1175-1190`).
- P2G 16.16 fixed point on the V1 route (bulk-water, water-realtime): contributions below ~7.6e-6 kg*m/s round to zero (verified for V1; not Iron Ice's field route).

Silent failure modes:
- A GPU authority rollback silently becomes a mechanics no-op (`sphMlsMpmGpuStep.js:13579-13582`, verified).
- One bad particle row cancels its level's dispatch with no error.
- `src/` and `ulg-gpu-abi/src` have no `uncapturederror` listener (verified by grep).
- Three executors report `'queue-work-completed'` without waiting (`peercomputeBrowserResidentHost.js:2105`, `:2347`, `:2592`).
- The 30 s stage timeout never cancels the schedule, so an orphan keeps advancing lane state without a commit (`ulgOffscreenRender.worker.js:696-706`).

Memory growth at high step rates: per-step `device.lost` handlers (fixed 2026-11-15); PeerCompute's `setHotBuffer` is a bare `Map.set` that never frees (`DataState.js:95`), keyed by step and time; caches keyed by particle count never pruned; render surface-summary kernels rebuild shader modules and pipelines on every call.

Fragile code generation:
- The live thermal shader is the ABI kernel plus 12 exact-text replacements with tab-indented anchors; reformatting breaks thermal at load (`wgsl.js:1270-1511`).
- V2 shaders are V1 plus string patches; several no-op silently on drift (the seam behind the `6bac50e` rollback bug).
- Import-time regexes throw on a miss, so an ABI edit can stop the worker loading.
- The reaction-extent law exists in seven copies (`interface_flux_extent_cap_mol` at `wgsl.js:2174`, `5680`, `6344`, `6775`, `7202`, `7499`, plus one made by regex). Capping only two once made 1,172 kg of NaOH from 127 kg of Na.
